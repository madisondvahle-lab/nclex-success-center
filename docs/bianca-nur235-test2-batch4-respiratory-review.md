## Proposed option and answer-key changes

Option IDs remain A–D or A–E; question IDs remain unchanged. These are draft
choices for review. Update correct and option-specific rationales if accepted.

### 081 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Record the first count as the patient's resting respiratory rate.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to slow the breathing while the nurse counts.”
- **C:** “Allow the patient to rest, then reassess rate, depth, pattern, effort, and symptoms.”
- **D:** “Diagnose a respiratory infection from the post-walk rate alone.”

**Rationale:** Recent activity can raise rate. Reassessment after rest helps
establish the patient's status without coaching the breathing or diagnosing
from one measurement.

### 082 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Record cough history and use it as a substitute for observing current breathing.”
- **B:** “Inspect symmetry, skin color, configuration, rate, depth, pattern, and visible effort.”
- **C:** “Inspect only the anterior chest because posterior symmetry is not relevant.”
- **D:** “Prioritize facial and eye features before observing respiratory effort.”

**Rationale:** Initial respiratory inspection directly observes the thorax and
breathing pattern, while history complements but does not replace observation.

### 083 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Treat any bronchial-quality sound over a peripheral field as proof of pneumonia.”
- **B:** “Expect bronchial sounds over all lung fields in a healthy adult.”
- **C:** “Move the stethoscope over clothing to reduce sound intensity.”
- **D:** “Recheck the site and compare with adjacent and opposite fields; a bronchial quality in a peripheral field is unexpected and needs clinical correlation.”

**Rationale:** Bronchial sounds are expected over the trachea, while vesicular
sounds are expected over most peripheral lung fields. An unexpected sound
location merits reassessment; it does not independently diagnose a cause.

### 084 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “Place the diaphragm on bare skin when possible.”
- **B:** “Compare corresponding areas side to side in a consistent sequence.”
- **C:** “Listen through a full respiratory cycle at each site when feasible.”
- **D:** “Listen to one posterior site and use it to represent both lungs.”
- **E:** “Ask the patient to breathe through the mouth and pause if dizziness occurs.”

**Rationale:** Bare-skin placement, paired comparison, a full respiratory
cycle, and pausing for dizziness support a systematic assessment. One site
cannot represent both lungs.

### 085 — retain key A; revise options

- **A:** “When does the cough occur, is it dry or productive, what is the sputum like, and are pain or blood present?”
- **B:** “Does the cough sound like an allergy, so I can record that as the cause?”
- **C:** “Can you avoid coughing until the rest of the exam is finished?”
- **D:** “Would you like me to document pneumonia as the likely diagnosis?”

**Rationale:** The open, focused prompt characterizes cough and sputum without
leading the patient or assigning a diagnosis.

### 086 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Finish the posterior percussion sequence before notifying the response team.”
- **B:** “Activate emergency assistance and prioritize airway assessment.”
- **C:** “Ask the patient to cough repeatedly and reassess after the routine exam.”
- **D:** “Document the sound as vesicular and continue auscultation.”

**Rationale:** Inspiratory stridor with retractions and difficulty speaking
signals potential upper-airway compromise; routine examination must not delay
emergency response.

### 087 — proposed key ACDE

- **A:** “New accessory-muscle use and suprasternal retractions.”
- **B:** “A comfortable patient speaking normally with an even pattern and no increased effort.”
- **C:** “Inability to speak a full sentence because of breathlessness.”
- **D:** “Markedly reduced air movement in a patient who is tiring.”
- **E:** “New cyanosis or decreasing level of alertness.”

**Rationale:** Increased work of breathing, speech limitation, tiring with
reduced air movement, and cyanosis or altered alertness warrant prompt
reassessment/escalation. A comfortable patient without increased effort is not
the same warning pattern.

### 088 — proposed key D

- **A:** “The pattern proves a pneumothorax because fremitus is increased.”
- **B:** “Bronchial sounds are expected over every peripheral lung field.”
- **C:** “Increased fremitus is the expected finding over a pleural effusion.”
- **D:** “The findings can occur with consolidation; correlate with the full examination and other clinical data.”

**Rationale:** Consolidation can increase transmission of voice sounds and
produce bronchial-quality sounds peripherally. Findings need correlation and
are not diagnostic in isolation.

### 089 — retarget to foundational percussion; proposed key C; difficulty `moderate`

**Stem:** “When percussing a healthy adult's posterior lung fields, which sound
is generally expected over an air-filled lung?”

- **A:** “Dullness, as over a fluid-filled pleural space.”
- **B:** “Flatness, as over a large muscle.”
- **C:** “Resonance over most air-filled lung fields.”
- **D:** “Tympany over the entire posterior thorax.”

**Rationale:** Resonance is generally expected over healthy air-filled lung.
Percussion should be interpreted with location and the rest of the
assessment. This foundational objective is directly aligned with slide 11 and
reduces duplication with #095.

### 090 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “Wheezes can occur with narrowed airways but do not by themselves prove asthma.”
- **B:** “Crackles may occur with fluid or reopening of small airways and need clinical correlation.”
- **C:** “Crackles at the bases prove heart failure without considering other findings.”
- **D:** “Decreased fremitus can occur when pleural air or fluid dampens sound transmission.”
- **E:** “Increased fremitus can occur over consolidated lung.”

**Rationale:** Breath sounds and fremitus contribute to a pattern but do not
identify every cause on their own.

### 091 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Place the patient flat to reproduce the orthopnea before checking oxygenation.”
- **B:** “Treat the crackles as pneumonia without assessing edema or position.”
- **C:** “Attribute new edema and orthopnea to aging and continue routine care.”
- **D:** “Maintain the position that eases breathing, assess oxygenation/vital signs, and promptly communicate the combined findings.”

**Rationale:** Orthopnea, crackles, and new edema are a concerning
cardiopulmonary pattern. Support breathing and escalate without provoking
dyspnea or diagnosing a cause.

### 092 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Interpret quieter wheezing as improvement and observe without reassessment.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to walk to improve ventilation before contacting anyone.”
- **C:** “Treat drowsiness, exhaustion, and minimal air movement as possible impending respiratory failure and activate emergency assistance.”
- **D:** “Wait for the next scheduled assessment because the cough has stopped.”

**Rationale:** Reduced wheeze with poor air movement and declining alertness
can reflect critically reduced airflow, not recovery. Immediate response is
needed.

### 093 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “Allergens such as pollen or dust mites.”
- **B:** “Respiratory infections.”
- **C:** “Exercise or cold, dry air for some patients.”
- **D:** “Every patient has the same asthma triggers, so an individual history is unnecessary.”
- **E:** “Tobacco smoke, other irritants, or stress for some patients.”

**Rationale:** Triggers vary. Ask about individual exposures and symptom
patterns; do not assume an identical trigger for everyone.

### 094 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Assume diminished breath sounds are harmless after surgery and wait for fever.”
- **B:** “Assess oxygenation, pain, mobility, and the prescribed pulmonary-care plan; support ordered breathing/mobility measures and report deterioration.”
- **C:** “Diagnose pneumonia and request antibiotics without further evaluation.”
- **D:** “Maintain immobility to avoid discomfort.”

**Rationale:** Surgery, shallow breathing, and immobility increase
atelectasis risk, but findings require assessment. Support prescribed
pulmonary hygiene and mobility and communicate deterioration.

### 095 — retain key A; revise options

- **A:** “The pattern is compatible with pleural effusion; communicate the findings. Drainage decisions and thoracentesis are performed by qualified clinicians.”
- **B:** “Increased fremitus is required to identify every pleural effusion.”
- **C:** “Dullness alone establishes that the fluid is infected.”
- **D:** “The nurse should insert a needle at the bedside to confirm the imaging.”

**Rationale:** Effusion may produce dullness, decreased breath sounds, and
decreased fremitus. These findings and imaging inform clinical care; the
nurse's role is assessment and communication, not performing an unauthorized
procedure.

### 096 — proposed key BCDE

- **A:** “Symmetric chest expansion and unchanged bilateral breath sounds in a comfortable patient.”
- **B:** “Acute worsening oxygenation or hemodynamic instability.”
- **C:** “Rapid emergency escalation while supporting airway and breathing.”
- **D:** “New asymmetry in chest expansion.”
- **E:** “Unilateral markedly diminished or absent breath sounds.”

**Rationale:** Acute hypoxemia/instability, asymmetric expansion, and unilateral
loss of breath sounds in the described scenario support urgent evaluation;
emergency response must not wait for a routine exam sequence.

### 097 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Complete the environmental history before assessing breathing.”
- **B:** “Place the patient flat and continue the interview.”
- **C:** “Assess airway, breathing, oxygenation, and bleeding while initiating urgent clinical escalation.”
- **D:** “Reassure the patient that blood-streaked sputum is always caused by dry air.”

**Rationale:** Hemoptysis with dyspnea requires prompt assessment and
escalation; do not delay for a complete history or make a benign assumption.

### 098 — proposed key D

- **A:** “A 1:1 AP-to-transverse diameter is expected in every healthy adult.”
- **B:** “Chest shape alone confirms emphysema.”
- **C:** “The finding is pectus excavatum.”
- **D:** “An increased AP diameter can occur with chronic hyperinflation; correlate with symptoms and the rest of the examination.”

**Rationale:** A barrel-shaped chest may occur with chronic hyperinflation,
but configuration alone does not diagnose emphysema.

### 099 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “A common resting-adult reference is 12–20 breaths/minute, interpreted with symptoms and baseline.”
- **B:** “A persistent rate near 25/minute in a resting older adult warrants assessment and context, not automatic attribution to age.”
- **C:** “Use the adult reference range for children regardless of age.”
- **D:** “Interpret rate with depth, pattern, effort, oxygenation, and clinical context.”
- **E:** “Recheck an elevated or irregular rate under consistent conditions and use age-appropriate references.”

**Rationale:** Reference ranges depend on age and assessment context. The deck
lists 12–20/min for adults and a possible 12–25/min range in older adults;
neither a single rate nor age alone identifies a cause.

### 100 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Mechanical ventilation proves pneumonia and identifies its organism.”
- **B:** “The new fever, purulent secretions, crackles, and increased oxygen need warrant prompt evaluation for possible ventilator-associated pneumonia.”
- **C:** “These findings are expected during ventilation and do not need reassessment.”
- **D:** “Crackles exclude pneumonia because pneumonia causes only wheezing.”

**Rationale:** The symptom cluster warrants evaluation. Ventilation is a risk
context, not by itself proof of infection, a diagnosis, or the organism.
