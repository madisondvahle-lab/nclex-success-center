## Proposed option and answer-key changes

The following are draft alternatives for instructor review. Option IDs remain
A–D or A–E; the bank's question IDs remain unchanged. Rationales and
option-specific rationales would need corresponding updates if a proposal is
accepted.

### 021 — proposed key C

Keep the stem's focused-history objective. Replace the options with parallel
interview approaches:

- **A:** “Has this same headache happened before, and what did you do for it?”
- **B:** “Would you describe the pain as a migraine so I can document the likely cause?”
- **C:** “When did it begin, where is it, what does it feel like, and what changes it?”
- **D:** “Would you prefer medication before we discuss the headache pattern?”

**Rationale:** An open, structured history characterizes the symptom without
leading the patient or assigning a cause before assessment.

### 022 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Record tenderness only and leave the area unexamined to avoid discomfort.”
- **B:** “Describe the location and visible/palpable features, ask about onset or injury, and compare gently with nearby areas.”
- **C:** “Document a cyst because a small tender scalp area is usually benign.”
- **D:** “Press repeatedly to determine whether the finding is superficial or deep.”

**Rationale:** Gentle description and relevant history are appropriate; the
nurse should not diagnose from palpation alone or cause unnecessary pain.

### 023 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Compare light-touch sensation on the forehead, cheeks, and jaw.”
- **B:** “Observe symmetry while the patient smiles and raises the eyebrows.”
- **C:** “Assess shoulder shrug and head rotation against resistance.”
- **D:** “Ask the patient to clench the jaw while palpating the masseter muscles.”

**Rationale:** Jaw clenching with masseter palpation assesses CN V motor
function. The other maneuvers assess sensory CN V or other cranial nerves.

### 024 — proposed key ABE

- **A:** “Ask the patient to smile and show the teeth.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to raise both eyebrows.”
- **C:** “Ask the patient to protrude the tongue and move it side to side.”
- **D:** “Compare light-touch sensation on each cheek.”
- **E:** “Ask the patient to close both eyes tightly while the nurse gently tests closure.”

**Rationale:** Smile, eyebrow raise, and tight eye closure assess CN VII motor
function. Tongue movement assesses CN XII; cheek sensation is primarily CN V.

### 025 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Inspect the teeth and oral mucosa while the patient keeps the jaw still.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to force the mouth open against resistance and measure the maximum.”
- **C:** “Observe and gently palpate both TMJs as the patient opens and closes the mouth; note symmetry, range, pain, and clicking.”
- **D:** “Auscultate the carotids and use the result to identify the cause of joint clicking.”

**Rationale:** The TMJ should be assessed during movement and not forced.
Carotid auscultation and oral inspection do not answer the reported joint
symptom.

### 026 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Complete a full cranial-nerve examination before contacting the response team.”
- **B:** “Activate the facility stroke response and establish the last-known-well time.”
- **C:** “Ask the patient to rest while a second nurse repeats the assessment in an hour.”
- **D:** “Request a routine outpatient evaluation after documenting possible Bell’s palsy.”

**Rationale:** Sudden facial asymmetry with arm drift and speech change warrants
immediate stroke evaluation. Do not delay the emergency response for a
comprehensive exam or assign a diagnosis.

### 027 — proposed key ABDE

Retain the red-flag objective and replace choices:

- **A:** “A sudden headache that reaches maximum intensity quickly.”
- **B:** “A new headache with fever and neck stiffness.”
- **C:** “A familiar mild headache that resolves with the patient's established plan and has no new features.”
- **D:** “A headache with new weakness, confusion, or visual loss.”
- **E:** “A new headache after a recent head injury.”

**Rationale:** Thunderclap onset, infection-associated symptoms, neurologic
changes, or headache after head injury require clinical evaluation rather than
routine self-care. This content is a clinical safety application beyond the
specific warning signs printed on the slides.

### 028 — proposed key D, conditional on course confirmation

If the instructor confirms giant cell arteritis is in course scope, retain the
stem and use more clinically relevant competing actions:

- **A:** “Arrange a routine appointment in several weeks because the episodes are brief.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to avoid chewing until a dental appointment is available.”
- **C:** “Reassess only if vision loss becomes constant.”
- **D:** “Arrange urgent same-day medical evaluation for the new headache, jaw fatigue, scalp tenderness, and visual symptoms.”

**Rationale:** The cluster in an older adult is concerning for giant cell
arteritis, which can threaten vision; transient symptoms should not be
reassured away. If the topic is not taught, replace rather than assess it as
course content.

### 029 — retain key A; revise distractors

- **A:** “Assess onset, blood pressure, visual and neurologic symptoms, then promptly notify the appropriate clinician.”
- **B:** “Use the trimester to classify the headache as expected and defer additional assessment.”
- **C:** “Recommend an over-the-counter medication before checking the patient's history or pregnancy plan.”
- **D:** “Schedule routine follow-up unless the headache remains after hormone levels stabilize.”

**Rationale:** The lecture's first-trimester statement does not explain away a
new severe headache with visual symptoms. Prompt assessment is appropriate;
medication choices must be individualized in pregnancy.

### 030 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Document the finding and wait for the next scheduled well-child visit.”
- **B:** “Attribute the appearance to normal molding without checking the infant's state or position.”
- **C:** “Gently assess whether the fontanel feels soft, then observe at home until tomorrow.”
- **D:** “Promptly escalate the persistent bulge and decreased arousability while assessing neurologic status and vital signs.”

**Rationale:** A persistently bulging fontanel in a calm, upright infant with
decreased responsiveness warrants prompt medical evaluation. Do not press
forcefully or delay escalation.

### 031 — proposed key ACDE

- **A:** “CN V: compare facial sensation and assess jaw clenching.”
- **B:** “CN VII: assess facial sensation by comparing light touch on the cheeks.”
- **C:** “CN XI: assess shoulder shrug and head turning against resistance.”
- **D:** “CN XII: assess tongue protrusion and side-to-side movement.”
- **E:** “CN II: assess visual acuity or visual fields.”

**Rationale:** CN V includes facial sensation and mastication; CN XI controls
shoulder/head-turning movement; CN XII controls tongue movement; CN II is the
optic nerve. CN VII controls facial expression, not facial sensation. Confirm
the course's specific testing maneuvers against the assigned handout.

### 032 — retain key A; revise distractors

- **A:** “Promptly assess blood pressure and associated symptoms and notify the obstetric clinician.”
- **B:** “Treat the swelling as routine pregnancy edema and defer assessment unless it becomes painful.”
- **C:** “Document melasma as the explanation for swelling and visual spots.”
- **D:** “Recommend limiting fluids and reassessing at the next routine visit.”

**Rationale:** Sudden facial/hand swelling with persistent headache and new
visual symptoms needs prompt assessment for hypertensive or other urgent
complications. Do not attribute edema to melasma or advise unprescribed fluid
restriction.

### 033 — proposed key B, conditional on course confirmation

If reduced facial expression in Parkinson disease is taught or assigned, use:

- **A:** “The fixed expression establishes depression even when the patient reports feeling comfortable.”
- **B:** “Reduced spontaneous facial movement can occur with Parkinson disease; compare with baseline and ask about mood directly.”
- **C:** “A chronic unchanged expression is diagnostic of a new stroke.”
- **D:** “Do not document facial movement if the patient denies discomfort.”

**Rationale:** Hypomimia can occur in Parkinson disease, but expression alone
does not establish mood or an acute neurologic event. Slide 14 names Parkinson
disease but does not visibly describe hypomimia; confirm this teaching point
before retaining it.

### 034 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “Ask when the asymmetry began and whether it is new or changing.”
- **B:** “Observe the face at rest and during smile, eyebrow raise, and eye closure.”
- **C:** “Assess for associated speech, limb-strength, sensation, vision, or alertness changes.”
- **D:** “Label the finding a stroke before establishing onset or assessing associated symptoms.”
- **E:** “Compare with prior descriptions or photographs when available and document the observed movement.”

**Rationale:** Establish onset/baseline, characterize movement, and assess for
associated neurologic findings. Comparison with prior information supports
interpretation; assigning a cause prematurely does not.

### 035 — proposed key C, conditional on course confirmation

If zoster ophthalmicus is within course scope, revise options:

- **A:** “Arrange routine follow-up next week because the rash is limited to the face.”
- **B:** “Apply pressure to the lesions and wait to see whether light sensitivity resolves.”
- **C:** “Arrange urgent same-day medical evaluation for the periocular vesicles and photophobia.”
- **D:** “Assess the TMJ alone because the pain is unilateral.”

**Rationale:** A vesicular rash near the eye with photophobia may involve
vision-threatening ocular disease and needs prompt evaluation. The deck's
visible text does not identify zoster; confirm course inclusion before using
this item to assess lecture alignment.

### 036 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Use one head-circumference measurement to diagnose the cause of the asymmetry.”
- **B:** “Reassure the caregiver that progressive change is expected molding.”
- **C:** “Apply pressure to the sutures to see whether the skull shape changes.”
- **D:** “Review birth and positioning history, inspect shape and sutures, trend age-appropriate head circumference, and report progressive change.”

**Rationale:** Interpret infant head shape in developmental and birth context;
trend objective measurements and report progression. Avoid forceful
manipulation or conclusions from a single measure.

### 037 — retarget to older-adult head/face changes; proposed key ACDE

Replace the cranial-nerve matching item with:

**Stem:** “Which findings may be observed as part of an older adult's
head-and-face assessment? Select all that apply.”

- **A:** “Reduced subcutaneous facial fullness.”
- **B:** “New unilateral facial weakness should be assumed to be a normal age change.”
- **C:** “Changes in hair.”
- **D:** “Tooth loss or changes in dentition.”
- **E:** “Some limitation in range of motion.”

**Rationale:** The slide 9 lifespan content lists loss of subcutaneous facial
fat, hair changes, tooth loss, and range-of-motion limitations. New focal
weakness is not a normal aging assumption and requires assessment.

### 038 — retarget to older-adult lifespan assessment; proposed key A

Replace the overlapping temporal-headache case with:

**Stem:** “During an older-adult head-and-face assessment, which interpretation
of gradual facial fullness and hair changes is most appropriate?”

- **A:** “These can occur with aging; document the findings and consider the patient's baseline and associated concerns.”
- **B:** “They establish a neurologic diagnosis and require no further history.”
- **C:** “They should be ignored because all head-and-face changes are expected in older adults.”
- **D:** “They are evidence of an acute stroke even when gradual and unchanged.”

**Rationale:** Slide 9 lists age-related changes in facial subcutaneous fat and
hair. Document findings in context; age alone neither proves a diagnosis nor
makes every change benign.

### 039 — proposed key B; retain basic documentation objective

- **A:** “Right facial nerve paralysis caused by Bell's palsy.”
- **B:** “Right lower facial movement is reduced during smile; onset, baseline, and associated findings are being assessed.”
- **C:** “Patient is uncooperative because the smile is asymmetric.”
- **D:** “Face normal because the patient can speak.”

**Rationale:** The best charting is objective, side-specific, and neutral.
Diagnosis requires clinical evaluation; speech alone does not exclude facial
weakness.

### 040 — retarget to focused assessment of a familiar first-trimester headache; proposed key BDE

Replace the overlapping emergency red-flag checklist with:

**Stem:** “A pregnant patient in the first trimester reports a mild headache
similar to a familiar pattern that improves with rest. Which actions are
appropriate? Select all that apply.”

- **A:** “Attribute the headache to pregnancy without asking further questions.”
- **B:** “Characterize onset, duration, severity, pattern, and response to usual measures.”
- **C:** “Reassure the patient that all first-trimester headaches resolve when hormone levels stabilize.”
- **D:** “Ask about visual changes, focal neurologic symptoms, blood pressure concerns, and other warning features.”
- **E:** “Review the patient's pregnancy-specific care plan and medication history; seek clinical guidance if symptoms are new, worsening, or concerning.”

**Rationale:** A familiar, mild symptom without new warning features differs
from the urgent presentations in #029 and #032, but still merits focused
assessment. Do not assume the symptom is benign solely because of trimester or
promise that it will resolve.
