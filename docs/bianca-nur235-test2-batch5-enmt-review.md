## Proposed option and answer-key changes

Option IDs remain A–D or A–E; question IDs remain unchanged. These are draft
choices for review. Update correct and option-specific rationales if accepted.

### 061 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Have you noticed changes in balance, tinnitus, pain or drainage, noise exposure, or medicines that may affect hearing?”
- **B:** “You have an ear infection, correct?”
- **C:** “When did the hearing change begin, is it one or both ears, and what related symptoms or exposures have you noticed?”
- **D:** “Does your hearing change when you swallow?”

**Rationale:** A neutral history explores onset, laterality, associated
symptoms, relevant noise/medication exposure, and functional impact without
presuming infection or age-related loss.

### 062 — proposed key B

- **A:** “An opaque, markedly bulging membrane with obscured landmarks.”
- **B:** “A gray, translucent membrane with visible landmarks and no bulging or perforation.”
- **C:** “A visible defect with drainage from the canal.”
- **D:** “A bright-red membrane in every healthy adult.”

**Rationale:** The lecture describes a gray, translucent tympanic membrane.
Color must be interpreted with landmarks, symptoms, and the full otoscopic
view.

### 063 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Pain with tragus movement establishes middle-ear infection without otoscopy.”
- **B:** “The finding is typical of presbycusis and does not require ear inspection.”
- **C:** “Tragus tenderness proves a tympanic-membrane perforation.”
- **D:** “Pain with gentle tragus or auricle movement can support external-canal inflammation; inspect and interpret with the history.”

**Rationale:** Tragus/auricle pain is associated with otitis externa but is
not independently diagnostic. Continue appropriate assessment.

### 064 — proposed key ABE

- **A:** “In a normal Rinne pattern, air conduction is heard longer than bone conduction.”
- **B:** “A Weber sound perceived equally in both ears is not lateralized.”
- **C:** “Weber lateralization alone identifies the type and side of hearing loss.”
- **D:** “A fixed two-second Rinne result proves normal hearing at every frequency.”
- **E:** “Interpret Weber lateralization with symptoms and other findings, such as Rinne, rather than alone.”

**Rationale:** Rinne compares air and bone conduction; Weber lateralization
must be interpreted with other findings and does not by itself identify the
type of loss.

### 065 — proposed key A

- **A:** “Possible right conductive loss; correlate with the full assessment and arrange further evaluation as indicated.”
- **B:** “Definite left sensorineural loss, proven by the right-sided Weber result.”
- **C:** “Normal bilateral hearing at every frequency.”
- **D:** “An equilibrium problem rather than a hearing-screening finding.”

**Rationale:** Weber lateralization to the right together with bone
conduction greater than air conduction on the right is consistent with
possible right conductive loss. Tuning-fork tests are screening maneuvers, not
a substitute for formal audiologic evaluation.

### 066 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Assume the change is inevitable with age and skip assessment.”
- **B:** “Tell the patient to listen more carefully in noisy rooms.”
- **C:** “Assess onset, function, exposures, medications, and communication needs; presbycusis is possible but should not be assumed.”
- **D:** “Speak from another room to test whether the patient is paying attention.”

**Rationale:** Presbycusis can affect high-frequency hearing and speech
understanding in background noise, but age alone does not establish the cause.
Use respectful, functional assessment.

### 067 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Ask the patient to close the eyes while standing unsupported at the bed edge.”
- **B:** “Explain the test, clear the area, guard closely, and stop if balance becomes unsafe.”
- **C:** “Perform the test while the patient walks down the hallway.”
- **D:** “Leave the patient alone so their balance response is natural.”

**Rationale:** Romberg assesses balance and requires guarding, especially
with reported dizziness or a recent fall. Mild sway alone must be interpreted
in context.

### 068 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “Avoid inserting objects into the ear canal because they can injure the canal or tympanic membrane.”
- **B:** “A cotton-tipped applicator can push cerumen deeper and contribute to impaction.”
- **C:** “Seek advice before using removal products if there is pain, drainage, sudden hearing change, or prior ear surgery.”
- **D:** “Use another person's prescription drops if wax does not come out.”
- **E:** “Clean the outer ear only; do not insert the cloth or applicator into the canal.”

**Rationale:** Cotton-tipped applicators can cause trauma or push cerumen
inward. Outer-ear care does not require inserting objects; symptoms or relevant
history should prompt appropriate advice.

### 069 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Irrigate the canal to verify whether the membrane is intact.”
- **B:** “Insert a swab to estimate the depth of the suspected defect.”
- **C:** “Instill any available ear drops before confirming the injury.”
- **D:** “Document symptoms and appearance, avoid water/instrumentation and unverified drops, and arrange clinical evaluation.”

**Rationale:** Possible perforation with pain, drainage, and reduced hearing
requires evaluation. Irrigation or instrumentation can worsen injury; drops
should be selected by a qualified clinician when membrane integrity is
uncertain.

### 070 — retain key A; revise alternatives

- **A:** “Inspect the external nose and septum, then compare airflow through one nostril at a time while gently occluding the other.”
- **B:** “Occlude both nostrils and ask the patient to inhale deeply through the nose.”
- **C:** “Insert a cotton applicator deeply to measure the blocked passage.”
- **D:** “Diagnose a deviated septum from the symptom report alone.”

**Rationale:** Inspect symmetry and septum, then compare patency side to side
without inserting an object or diagnosing from symptoms alone.

### 071 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Tenderness establishes bacterial sinusitis and an antibiotic indication.”
- **B:** “Pressure and congestion establish a nasal polyp.”
- **C:** “The symptoms can occur with sinus inflammation; assess duration and associated symptoms before determining the cause.”
- **D:** “Sinus tenderness is always normal and does not need assessment.”

**Rationale:** Facial pressure, congestion, and tenderness are not specific
for bacterial infection; treatment decisions require the full clinical
picture.

### 072 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “Inspect the external nose for symmetry and skin changes.”
- **B:** “Compare airflow through each nostril separately.”
- **C:** “Assume allergies are the cause without asking about duration or triggers.”
- **D:** “Ask about duration, triggers, allergies, trauma, medications, and bleeding pattern.”
- **E:** “If bleeding is active, assess its pattern and the patient's overall status.”

**Rationale:** External inspection, one-sided patency comparison, focused
history, and assessment of active bleeding support safe evaluation. Do not
instrument the nose unnecessarily or assume a cause.

### 073 — proposed key B, conditional on course confirmation

- **A:** “Tilt the head back and swallow the blood while waiting.”
- **B:** “Sit upright, lean slightly forward, and apply continuous pressure to the soft part of the nose.”
- **C:** “Lie flat and insert tissue deeply into the nostril.”
- **D:** “Blow the nose forcefully until bleeding stops.”

**Rationale:** For an uncomplicated anterior nosebleed, forward posture and
continuous compression of the soft part of the nose are recommended. Escalate
persistent or severe bleeding. This is a clinically verified supplemental
first-aid concept, not explicit in the visible lecture text.

### 074 — proposed key D, conditional on course confirmation

- **A:** “Stop the prescribed inhaled corticosteroid immediately without consulting the prescriber.”
- **B:** “Scrape the patches forcefully to confirm candidiasis.”
- **C:** “Assume every white patch is harmless food residue.”
- **D:** “Document the appearance and symptoms, ask about inhaler/mouth-rinsing technique, and arrange evaluation.”

**Rationale:** White patches and soreness can be consistent with candidiasis,
and inhaled corticosteroids can increase risk, but assessment and evaluation
are needed. Do not stop prescribed therapy independently. Confirm the
inhaler-related teaching is in course scope.

### 075 — proposed key A, conditional on course confirmation

- **A:** “Activate emergency assistance and prioritize airway evaluation.”
- **B:** “Offer oral fluids and ask the patient to gargle.”
- **C:** “Complete a detailed dental history before responding.”
- **D:** “Document uncomplicated tonsillitis and schedule routine follow-up.”

**Rationale:** Drooling, inability to manage secretions, and inspiratory
stridor suggest possible upper-airway compromise. Immediate escalation takes
priority over oral intake or routine examination. Confirm that this
application is taught or assigned.

### 076 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “Inspect the lips, oral mucosa, gums, teeth, tongue, and visible throat.”
- **B:** “Note color, lesions or white patches, odor, and presence or absence of tonsils.”
- **C:** “Assume a white patch is candidiasis without describing it or obtaining context.”
- **D:** “Ask about pain, swallowing difficulty, oral hygiene, and relevant dental or medication history.”
- **E:** “If the patient has stridor or cannot manage secretions, prioritize airway help over routine inspection.”

**Rationale:** Inspect and describe the oral/throat structures and gather
focused history. Airway compromise takes precedence over the routine
assessment.

### 077 — proposed key C, conditional on course confirmation

- **A:** “Offer a larger sip to determine whether the cough can be reproduced.”
- **B:** “Document normal swallowing because the patient can speak.”
- **C:** “Stop oral intake, assess breathing and swallowing safety, and promptly notify the appropriate clinician.”
- **D:** “Place the patient supine and continue the meal.”

**Rationale:** Coughing with liquids and a wet-sounding voice can indicate
impaired swallowing and aspiration risk. Stop the trial, ensure respiratory
stability, and arrange further evaluation rather than challenging swallowing
again. Confirm the aspiration-management detail is in course scope.

### 078 — proposed key ACDE

- **A:** “Ask about duration, progression, fever, pain, swallowing, and voice changes.”
- **B:** “Assume intermittent hoarseness is caused by allergy without further history.”
- **C:** “Ask whether the patient has breathing difficulty, drooling, or inability to swallow secretions.”
- **D:** “Ask about tobacco/irritant exposure, reflux symptoms, and relevant environmental exposures.”
- **E:** “Ask about medication and inhaler use and any associated oral symptoms.”

**Rationale:** Focused history includes the symptom pattern, airway/swallowing
warning signs, exposures, and relevant medicines. Do not infer cause from
ancestry or a single symptom. The slide 7 ancestry statement should not be
generalized; use patient-specific oral health history and access needs.

### 079 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Diagnose bacterial sinusitis from bilateral congestion alone.”
- **B:** “Acknowledge that pregnancy-related vascular changes can contribute; assess severity and associated symptoms and check pregnancy-specific guidance before medication advice.”
- **C:** “Dismiss the symptom because the external nose appears normal.”
- **D:** “Recommend any decongestant without reviewing pregnancy-specific risks.”

**Rationale:** Increased vascularity in pregnancy can contribute to
congestion, but the nurse should assess symptoms and avoid assuming infection.
Medication guidance should be individualized.

### 080 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “Dysphagia means difficulty swallowing.”
- **B:** “Presbycusis can affect high-frequency hearing and speech understanding in noise.”
- **C:** “A normal Romberg result proves normal hearing acuity.”
- **D:** “Weber lateralization needs interpretation with clinical context and other hearing findings.”
- **E:** “Oral patches should be described and assessed in context; color alone does not establish thrush.”

**Rationale:** Dysphagia is a swallowing term; presbycusis affects hearing;
Weber assesses sound lateralization, while Romberg assesses equilibrium.
Oral findings require context.
