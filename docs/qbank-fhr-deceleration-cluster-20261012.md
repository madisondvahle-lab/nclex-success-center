**Clinical guidance**: ACOG Clinical Practice Guideline No. 10 (October 2025) supports individualized evaluation and intrauterine resuscitative measures (maternal position changes, fluid bolus for indicated hypovolemia, reducing induction agents, amnioinfusion when appropriate); **does not recommend routine maternal oxygen in the absence of hypoxia**. Variation in category II tracings means neither every variable deceleration nor a fixed time limit warrants immediate cesarean delivery.

## Replacement #43 — Initial response (general NCLEX)
A laboring patient at 39 weeks has recurrent variable fetal heart rate decelerations. Baseline is 140/min with moderate variability, the patient is supine, and maternal SpO2 is 99% on room air. Which nursing intervention is most appropriate first?
- A. Apply a nonrebreather oxygen mask despite normal maternal oxygenation.
- B. Change the maternal position laterally and reassess fetal heart rate and uterine activity.
- C. Increase oxytocin to shorten labor.
- D. Arrange immediate cesarean birth without initial supportive measures.

**Key: B.** A routine supplemental oxygen has no demonstrated benefit without maternal hypoxia. **B** may relieve intermittent cord compression and allows evaluation of the tracing. C may worsen excessive uterine activity. D is premature without context suggesting failure of appropriate measures or more ominous tracing characteristics.
**Goal:** Prioritize correct first-line action; avoid outdated oxygen reflex.

## Replacement #101 — Reassessment and escalation (general NCLEX)
A patient in active labor has recurrent variable decelerations despite lateral repositioning. Baseline fetal heart rate is 150/min and moderate variability is present; the tracing remains category II. Contractions occur every 2–3 minutes, maternal SpO2 is 98%, and a clinician is available at bedside. Which nursing action best supports the next clinical decision?
- A. Report the persistence and characteristics of the pattern, assess uterine activity and maternal status, and collaborate on further intrauterine resuscitation such as amnioinfusion when appropriate.
- B. Apply supplemental oxygen routinely because any category II tracing requires oxygen.
- C. Document the pattern as normal because moderate variability makes all decelerations harmless.
- D. Increase the oxytocin rate to resolve cord compression.

**Key: A.** **A** uses serial assessment and provider collaboration; amnioinfusion may be considered for recurrent variable decelerations when appropriate and ordered. B applies oxygen without hypoxemia. C falsely equates reassuring variability with no need for assessment. D is not an effective strategy and may worsen the tracing.
**Goal:** Assessment of persistence and response to first-line measures; avoid duplicate recognition item.

## Revision #162 — Contextualize cord-prolapse screening
Keep the correct answer provisionally but modify the scenario to include **spontaneous rupture of membranes followed by new abrupt recurrent variable decelerations** if assessing for possible prolapse is intended. The nurse should promptly evaluate the tracing, reposition and assess for signs of cord prolapse according to obstetric emergency protocol. An isolated category II variable pattern does not establish cord prolapse.
**Editorial note:** Rewrite competing options after scenario revision and confirm one best first action.

## Rationale correction #306 (course-specific)
Retain key C and course separation. Proposed rationale: Abrupt variable decelerations commonly reflect intermittent umbilical cord compression. Maternal repositioning is a reasonable initial action while reassessing the overall tracing, uterine activity and maternal-fetal status. Further measures depend on clinical findings, including oxytocin use, hypotension or persistent recurrent decelerations. Supplemental oxygen is **not** routine when maternal saturation is normal. Following recent membrane rupture, consider cord prolapse if the pattern and examination raise concern.
