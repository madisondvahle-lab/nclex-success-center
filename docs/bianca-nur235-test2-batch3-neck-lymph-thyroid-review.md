## Proposed option and answer-key changes

Option IDs remain A–D or A–E; question IDs remain unchanged. These are draft
choices for instructor review. Update correct and option-specific rationales
if accepted.

### 041 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Diagnose tension pneumothorax from the initial visual impression.”
- **B:** “Press firmly on the trachea until it appears centered.”
- **C:** “Ignore the finding because tracheal position is not clinically relevant.”
- **D:** “Reposition the patient, reinspect, palpate gently, and interpret the finding with symptoms and baseline.”

**Rationale:** Verify a subtle observation before labeling it; acute deviation
with distress is a different, urgent presentation.

### 042 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Use the thumb to press firmly into each node until its borders are clear.”
- **B:** “Use finger pads in gentle circular motions, palpating node groups in sequence and comparing sides.”
- **C:** “Assess only the supraclavicular group because other cervical nodes cannot be palpated.”
- **D:** “Pinch each node sharply between the fingertips to test consistency.”

**Rationale:** Gentle, systematic palpation supports comparison and
characterization without unnecessary discomfort.

### 043 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Ask the patient to turn the head against resistance while watching the thyroid.”
- **B:** “Ask the patient to cough repeatedly while the nurse palpates both carotids.”
- **C:** “Ask the patient to take a sip of water and swallow while observing the lower anterior neck.”
- **D:** “Ask the patient to hold the breath and shrug both shoulders.”

**Rationale:** The thyroid region moves with swallowing; the other maneuvers
do not assess that movement.

### 044 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “Location and approximate size.”
- **B:** “Tenderness and consistency.”
- **C:** “Mobility or fixation relative to surrounding tissue.”
- **D:** “A definitive diagnosis of malignancy based only on palpation.”
- **E:** “Distribution, nearby node involvement, and relevant associated symptoms.”

**Rationale:** Record objective node characteristics and context; palpation
alone does not diagnose malignancy.

### 045 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Move the patient's head passively through the full range before asking about pain.”
- **B:** “Use rapid repeated neck circles to measure endurance.”
- **C:** “Limit the assessment to rotation because other movements are not part of neck range.”
- **D:** “Ask the patient to move actively through comfortable flexion, extension, rotation, and lateral bending; stop if pain or concerning symptoms occur.”

**Rationale:** Active movement within comfort preserves patient control and
avoids forcing a painful or symptomatic neck.

### 046 — retain key A; revise distractors

- **A:** “Activate emergency assistance and support immediate evaluation for a life-threatening thoracic complication.”
- **B:** “Repeat the complete neck examination before activating the emergency response.”
- **C:** “Reposition the patient and wait for the next scheduled oxygen-saturation measurement.”
- **D:** “Attribute the deviation to anatomy because the patient had a central-line procedure.”

**Rationale:** Acute respiratory distress, unilateral absent breath sounds,
falling oxygenation, and tracheal deviation after a central-line procedure
require immediate escalation. Do not delay for a complete routine exam.

### 047 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “When indicated and within training, listen over the carotids before palpation and ask the patient to briefly hold the breath.”
- **B:** “Palpate one carotid artery at a time with gentle pressure.”
- **C:** “Avoid massaging or compressing the carotid sinus.”
- **D:** “Compress both carotid arteries simultaneously to compare pulse strength.”
- **E:** “Ask about relevant symptoms or prior carotid procedures and stop if concerning symptoms occur.”

**Rationale:** Avoid bilateral compression and carotid-sinus pressure. Keep
assessment within the clinician's training and context.

### 048 — proposed key C

Revise the stem to specify: “At 45 degrees, the nurse observes a sustained
jugular venous column above the sternal angle in a patient with orthopnea.”

- **A:** “Call the venous pulsation a carotid bruit without using auscultation.”
- **B:** “Press on both jugular veins to stop the pulsation and confirm the cause.”
- **C:** “Document the position and venous finding, assess related cardiopulmonary signs, and promptly communicate the pattern.”
- **D:** “Lower the patient flat and leave the room to see whether the finding persists.”

**Rationale:** Describe the finding and position, assess the full clinical
picture, and communicate the concerning pattern without unsafe manipulation.

### 049 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Classify the fullness as normal because the thyroid region rises during swallowing.”
- **B:** “Document the enlargement and new dysphagia and arrange further clinical evaluation.”
- **C:** “Press deeply on the gland to reproduce the swallowing difficulty.”
- **D:** “Diagnose a specific thyroid hormone disorder from appearance alone.”

**Rationale:** Thyroid movement with swallowing can be observed, but visible
enlargement with new difficulty swallowing requires further evaluation.

### 050 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “Inspect the anterior neck and observe movement during swallowing.”
- **B:** “When indicated and within training, palpate gently for enlargement, asymmetry, nodules, or tenderness.”
- **C:** “Use forceful pressure over the gland to make a bruit easier to hear.”
- **D:** “When enlargement or the clinical context warrants it, an appropriately trained clinician may auscultate for a bruit.”
- **E:** “Document findings and communicate abnormalities rather than treating a bruit as a diagnosis.”

**Rationale:** Inspection, gentle indicated palpation, and context-dependent
auscultation are distinct assessment steps; none alone establishes a
diagnosis. Preserve the deck's advanced-skill qualification.

### 051 — proposed key D

- **A:** “Reassure the patient that a fixed node is a routine age-related change.”
- **B:** “Massage the node repeatedly to see whether it becomes mobile.”
- **C:** “Tell the patient the finding proves cancer.”
- **D:** “Document size, location, tenderness, consistency, and mobility, then arrange prompt clinician evaluation.”

**Rationale:** A firm, fixed supraclavicular node is concerning and warrants
evaluation; palpation alone cannot establish a diagnosis.

### 052 — proposed key C

- **A:** “Tenderness establishes a bacterial infection and confirms that antibiotics are needed.”
- **B:** “A recent sore throat makes further assessment unnecessary.”
- **C:** “The pattern may be reactive; assess the throat and associated symptoms and follow up if nodes persist, enlarge, or worsen.”
- **D:** “The nurse should diagnose malignancy because multiple nodes are palpable.”

**Rationale:** Tender mobile nodes after a local infection can be reactive, but
the cause is not certain from palpation. Assess context and persistence.

### 053 — proposed key BCDE

- **A:** “Preauricular nodes are located behind the ear near the mastoid.”
- **B:** “Postauricular nodes are located behind the ear near the mastoid region.”
- **C:** “Submental nodes are located beneath the chin.”
- **D:** “Supraclavicular nodes are located above the clavicle.”
- **E:** “The deep cervical chain lies along deeper structures of the neck.”

**Rationale:** The named groups correspond to their anatomic landmarks.
Preauricular means anterior to the ear; deep cervical nodes are not axillary.

### 054 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Assume a viral infection and advise follow-up only if the nodes become tender.”
- **B:** “Document the distribution and characteristics and promptly notify the clinician for further evaluation.”
- **C:** “Tell the patient the findings establish lymphoma.”
- **D:** “Massage the nodes and reassess them at a routine visit several months later.”

**Rationale:** Multiple enlarged nodes and constitutional symptoms need
clinical evaluation; communicate observations without assigning a diagnosis.

### 055 — retain key A; revise distractors

- **A:** “Treat this as a potential airway emergency and activate immediate clinical assistance.”
- **B:** “Complete node and thyroid palpation first so the cause is clearer.”
- **C:** “Offer food or water to check whether the swallowing problem improves.”
- **D:** “Arrange routine outpatient imaging and continue the assessment.”

**Rationale:** Noisy breathing and difficulty handling secretions with a
rapidly enlarging mass can indicate airway compromise; do not delay escalation
or offer oral intake.

### 056 — proposed key ABCE

- **A:** “A new fixed supraclavicular node.”
- **B:** “A rapidly enlarging thyroid-region mass with new dysphagia.”
- **C:** “Acute tracheal deviation accompanied by respiratory distress.”
- **D:** “A small, mobile, mildly tender node during a short-lived URI, with no other concerns.”
- **E:** “A new neck mass with hoarseness or other progressive compressive symptoms.”

**Rationale:** Fixed nodes, progressive masses with compressive symptoms, and
acute respiratory distress require communication or further evaluation. A
small, tender, mobile node during a brief URI may be reactive but still merits
contextual follow-up.

### 057 — proposed key C, conditional on training

- **A:** “Document definite hyperthyroidism based on the bruit.”
- **B:** “Ignore the bruit because thyroid auscultation is never appropriate.”
- **C:** “Document the bruit and enlargement and communicate the findings; the bruit alone does not establish a diagnosis.”
- **D:** “Press harder on the gland until the sound disappears.”

**Rationale:** A bruit is an assessment finding, not a standalone diagnosis.
Retain this item only for learners taught the advanced maneuver.

### 058 — proposed key D

- **A:** “A thyroid nodule, because every neck pulsation rises and falls with breathing.”
- **B:** “A carotid bruit, which is identified by visual inspection alone.”
- **C:** “A cervical lymph node that expands with each heartbeat.”
- **D:** “A jugular venous pulsation; assess its position and relation to other findings.”

**Rationale:** A soft, undulating pulsation that changes with position is
consistent with jugular venous pulsation, not an auscultated bruit or node.

### 059 — proposed key ABDE

- **A:** “The thyroid region may move with swallowing, but enlargement or compressive symptoms still need assessment.”
- **B:** “Tenderness can occur in a reactive node but does not identify the cause by itself.”
- **C:** “Tenderness proves that a node is bacterial and requires antibiotics.”
- **D:** “A firm, fixed node is more concerning than a small mobile node, but palpation alone is not diagnostic.”
- **E:** “New dysphagia, hoarseness, or breathing difficulty with a neck mass warrants prompt evaluation.”

**Rationale:** Interpret findings in context, avoid diagnosing from palpation
or bruit alone, and escalate compressive symptoms.

### 060 — proposed key B

- **A:** “Assume the swelling is infection and apply heat before assessing the limb.”
- **B:** “Compare both limbs, characterize swelling and skin, ask about pain/redness/warmth and change, and arrange clinical follow-up.”
- **C:** “Massage forcefully toward the chest to move fluid.”
- **D:** “Document the swelling as inevitable because lymph-node surgery always causes it.”

**Rationale:** Prior node removal raises the possibility of lymphedema but
does not establish the cause of new unilateral swelling. Compare and
characterize the finding; acute pain, warmth, redness, rapid change, or
systemic symptoms would alter urgency.
