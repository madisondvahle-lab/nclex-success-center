## Q33 — DKA: potassium before insulin (P0)
**Stem:** An adult admitted with diabetic ketoacidosis has received an initial isotonic fluid bolus. Glucose is 486 mg/dL, potassium 3.0 mEq/L, pH 7.19, and bicarbonate 12 mEq/L. Which new prescription should the nurse question?
- **A.** Initiate continuous cardiac monitoring.
- **B.** Begin prescribed IV potassium replacement with appropriate monitoring.
- **C.** Start an IV regular insulin infusion immediately.
- **D.** Continue prescribed isotonic fluid resuscitation.
**Key: C.**
**Option rationale:** A appropriate for dysrhythmia risk. B appropriate to correct severe potassium deficit. **C unsafe:** insulin moves potassium intracellularly; delay until potassium is restored to above 3.5 mmol/L per 2024 consensus. D appropriate with clinical reassessment.
**Takeaway:** DKA treatment sequences depend on potassium, not just hyperglycemia.
**Reference:** https://doi.org/10.2337/dci24-0032

## Q85 — Neonatal resuscitation (P0)
**Stem:** A term newborn is dried, positioned, and stimulated immediately after birth. The infant continues to breathe ineffectively, and heart rate remains 90/min. What is the priority intervention?
- **A.** Continue tactile stimulation for another minute.
- **B.** Initiate positive-pressure ventilation.
- **C.** Start chest compressions before ventilating.
- **D.** Administer supplemental oxygen alone and observe.
**Key: B.**
**Option rationale:** A delays indicated ventilation. **B indicated** for persistent heart rate below 100/min after initial steps or ineffective breathing. C compressions are reserved for persistent HR below 60/min after effective ventilation. D oxygen alone does not address ventilation failure.
**Takeaway:** Clinical assessment, not an APGAR total, determines resuscitation.
**Reference:** https://publications.aap.org/pediatrics/article/157/1/e2025074352/205237/Part-5-Neonatal-Resuscitation-2025-American-Heart

## Q38 — APGAR: interpret without directing resuscitation from score (P0)
**Stem:** A newborn's 1-minute APGAR score is 5. The nurse is documenting the score while another clinician continues evaluating breathing and heart rate. Which interpretation is accurate?
- **A.** The score describes the newborn's condition at 1 minute; resuscitation is based on breathing and heart rate.
- **B.** A score of 5 automatically requires supplemental oxygen, whether or not the newborn is breathing adequately.
- **C.** A score of 5 confirms hypoxic-ischemic brain injury.
- **D.** The team should wait until the 5-minute APGAR score before providing necessary ventilation.
**Key: A.**
**Option rationale:** **A correct**: APGAR communicates condition and response to care; it does not set resuscitation decisions. B score alone cannot determine oxygen need. C APGAR is not a standalone diagnostic test for brain injury. D resuscitation must not wait for scoring.
**Reference:** https://www.acog.org/clinical/clinical-guidance/committee-opinion/articles/2015/10/the-apgar-score

## Q101 — Variable decelerations (P0)
**Stem:** A laboring patient has recurrent abrupt variable fetal heart rate decelerations with moderate baseline variability. Maternal oxygen saturation is 98% on room air; the patient is supine. Which nursing action is appropriate first?
- **A.** Apply oxygen by nonrebreather despite normal maternal saturation.
- **B.** Reposition the patient laterally and reassess the fetal tracing.
- **C.** Increase the oxytocin infusion to accelerate delivery.
- **D.** Stop fetal monitoring while obtaining an ultrasound.
**Key: B.**
**Option rationale:** A routine oxygen is not indicated with normal maternal oxygenation solely for FHR abnormality. **B correct**: maternal repositioning may relieve cord compression; reassess response and escalate per fetal status. C may worsen uterine activity and fetal tolerance. D interrupts continuous assessment.
**Takeaway:** Treat underlying contributors; tailor interventions to maternal and fetal findings.
**Reference:** ACOG practice advisory on oxygen supplementation with category II/III tracings; check institutional algorithm at sign-off.

## Q55 — Excessive newborn weight loss (P1)
**Stem:** At a day-4 outpatient assessment, a breastfed newborn is 11% below birth weight, has had two wet diapers in 24 hours, and repeatedly falls asleep shortly after latching. Which action is most appropriate?
- **A.** Reassure the parent that losses up to 12% require no follow-up.
- **B.** Schedule the next weight assessment at 2 weeks.
- **C.** Arrange prompt clinical evaluation of hydration, milk transfer, and feeding effectiveness.
- **D.** Tell the parent that diaper count confirms adequate feeding.
**Key: C.**
**Option rationale:** A excessive loss needs assessment. B delaying evaluation risks dehydration and inadequate intake. **C correct** based on convergent weight, output, and feeding cues. D low output is not reassuring.
**Reference:** https://www.aap.org/en/patient-care/newborn-infant-and-early-childhood-nutrition/newborn-and-infant-health-assessment-and-promotion/first-office-visit-3-5-days/

## Q29 — Preeclampsia without proteinuria (P1)
**Stem:** At 31 weeks' gestation, a patient has newly elevated blood pressure of 152/96 and 150/94 mm Hg four hours apart. Urine protein testing is negative. Platelets are 82,000/mm³, with no known alternative cause of thrombocytopenia. Which interpretation is most appropriate?
- **A.** Preeclampsia is excluded without proteinuria.
- **B.** The findings represent uncomplicated gestational hypertension.
- **C.** The findings support preeclampsia with severe features.
- **D.** Preeclampsia cannot be considered before 34 weeks.
**Key: C.**
**Option rationale:** A incorrect; new hypertension plus qualifying organ dysfunction can establish diagnosis. B platelets below 100,000 are a severe feature. **C correct** with a newly hypertensive patient and marked thrombocytopenia. D condition may develop after 20 weeks.
**Reference:** https://www.acog.org/community/districts-and-sections/district-iv/whats-new/countdown-to-intern-year-week-3-hypertensive-disorders
