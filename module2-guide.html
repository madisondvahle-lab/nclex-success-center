Module Overview

What this module covers and why it matters

Maternal and OB nursing is one of the highest-yield areas on the NCLEX. It tests your ability to prioritize the mother's safety, interpret fetal monitoring strips, recognize obstetric emergencies, and act fast — before harm reaches mom or baby.

The guiding principle of this entire module is "Mom First, Then Baby." When you're unsure of the priority, start there. Assess, recognize, prioritize, and ACT.

The Big Picture

Frame the content before you dive in

What the NCLEX tests

Fetal monitoring interpretation · When to notify the provider · Obstetric emergency priority actions · Medication safety (Mag sulfate) · Safe vs. live vaccines

The core concept

Pregnancy is a normal physiologic state — but complications develop fast. Your job is to recognize danger EARLY and intervene before mom or baby deteriorates.

The #1 rule

Mom first, then baby. Stabilize the mother before shifting focus to the fetus. Every priority question in OB starts here.

The #1 NCLEX trap here

Confusing late decelerations (placental insufficiency = ominous) with early decelerations (head compression = benign). The timing is everything.

💡 Madison's Rule: When you see a fetal heart rate question, ask yourself — does the dip START with the contraction (early = benign) or AFTER the peak (late = emergency)? That one question will carry you through most FHR NCLEX questions.

Memory Tricks

Anchors built to stick

L
Late decels: "STOP the drip, LEFT side, O₂ and fluids"Stop Pitocin → Left lateral → O₂ → IVF → Notify provider. In that order, every time.
P
Previa vs. Abruption: "Previa = Painless + Pink (bright red). Abruption = Awful pain + dark"Previa = placenta is blocking the exit (painless bright bleeding). Abruption = placenta rips away (painful, dark, rigid uterus).
M
Mag Toxicity: "RUO" — Respirations, Urinary output, DTRs (reflexes)If resp <12, UO <30 mL/hr, or DTRs absent → STOP mag + give calcium gluconate.
H
HELLP: "Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets"Think of the liver screaming: "HELL P!" — RUQ pain + nausea/vomiting + malaise are the classic symptoms.
G
GTPAL: "Got Tummy? Pretty Amazing Living children"Gravida · Term · Preterm · Abortions · Living. G = total pregnancies (includes current).
R
RhoGAM: "72 hours — or it's too late to Rhogam"Must be given within 72 hours postpartum. Does NOT work if mom is already sensitized (positive antibody screen).

NCLEX Pearls

Always-true statements for the exam

💎
Early decelerations = head compression = benign. No intervention needed.
💎
Late decelerations = placental insufficiency = OMINOUS. Reposition left lateral, O₂, IVF, stop Pitocin, notify provider.
💎
Variable decelerations = cord compression. First action = reposition. Then O₂ and fluids if persistent.
💎
Mag sulfate toxicity: Stop infusion + give calcium gluconate IV. Keep calcium gluconate at bedside for any patient on Mag.
💎
Placenta previa: NEVER perform a vaginal exam — can cause massive hemorrhage. Bed rest, monitor, IV access.
💎
Cord prolapse: Knee-chest position immediately. Manually lift presenting part off cord. DO NOT push cord back in.
💎
RhoGAM must be given within 72 hours postpartum. It is ineffective if mom is already sensitized (positive indirect Coombs).
💎
GDM screening occurs at 24–28 weeks. 1-hr glucose screen ≥140 → 3-hr GTT. Insulin is preferred over oral agents in pregnancy.
💎
Fundal height at umbilicus = 20 weeks. This is the one measurement the NCLEX always tests.
💎
20 weeks fundal height = 20 weeks gestation (in cm, fundal height roughly equals weeks from 20–36 weeks).

NCLEX Traps

Where students go wrong — and why

⚠️
Early vs. Late decelerations. Both dip during contractions — but early decels mirror the contraction, late decels lag behind. Timing = everything.
⚠️
RhoGAM when already sensitized. A positive indirect Coombs means mom is ALREADY sensitized — RhoGAM is contraindicated. It prevents sensitization, not treats it.
⚠️
Vaginal exam with placenta previa. This is always WRONG. Any vaginal examination can rupture the placenta and cause fatal hemorrhage.
⚠️
Pushing the cord back in during cord prolapse. Never do this — it causes vasospasm. Knee-chest + lift presenting part + emergent delivery.
⚠️
HELLP without severe HTN. HELLP can present WITHOUT markedly elevated BP. RUQ pain + malaise + low platelets = suspect HELLP regardless of BP.
⚠️
Calcium gluconate vs. calcium chloride for Mag toxicity. The antidote is calcium GLUCONATE (not chloride). Calcium gluconate is what you keep at the bedside.
⚠️
FluMist in pregnancy. Injectable flu vaccine = safe. FluMist (live attenuated nasal spray) = AVOID in pregnancy. Live vaccines are always contraindicated.
⚠️
GTPAL: Gravida vs. Para. Gravida = total pregnancies (includes current). A woman at 30 weeks who has 2 living children and had 1 prior loss is G4 (current + 3 previous pregnancies).

Clinical Connections

How this shows up at the bedside

📞 Call the Provider When...
  • BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks
  • Severe headache or visual changes
  • RUQ or epigastric pain
  • Proteinuria 2+ or greater
  • Recurrent late decelerations
  • Any vaginal bleeding in pregnancy
  • Decreased fetal movement after 28 wks
  • Preterm contractions (<37 weeks)
  • Signs of mag toxicity
⚡ Immediate Priority Actions
  • Late decels → STOP Pitocin, left lateral, O₂
  • Cord prolapse → Knee-chest, lift presenting part
  • Mag toxicity → Stop infusion, calcium gluconate
  • Placenta previa → NO vaginal exam, bed rest
  • Eclamptic seizure → Side-lying, protect from injury, time it, call for help
🔬 Lab Values to Know
  • Proteinuria ≥300 mg/24hr = preeclampsia
  • Platelets <100,000 = HELLP concern
  • 1-hr glucose ≥140 mg/dL = abnormal
  • Mag therapeutic level: 4–8 mEq/L
  • Mag toxic level: >9 mEq/L
  • Dilutional anemia (↓ Hgb/Hct) = NORMAL in pregnancy
👶 Fetal Status Indicators
  • Accelerations = reassuring ("happy baby")
  • Minimal/absent variability = concerning
  • Kick counts: 10 movements in 2 hrs
  • BPP score ≥8 = reassuring
  • Late decels + absent variability = highest priority

Priority Framework

How to sequence your thinking in OB NCLEX questions

1
Mom FirstStabilize the mother before addressing fetal status. Assess maternal airway, BP, level of consciousness, and bleeding first.
2
Recognize the EmergencyIs this cord prolapse? Abruption? Eclampsia? Mag toxicity? Naming the problem drives the action. Don't act before you know what you're treating.
3
Stop the HarmStop Pitocin for late decels. Stop mag for toxicity. Stop vaginal exam for previa. Removing the cause comes before adding the intervention.
4
Position + OxygenateLeft lateral = relieves aorto-caval compression. O₂ by face mask = improves fetal and maternal oxygenation. These are almost always the next steps.
5
Notify + PrepareNotify the provider, establish IV access, draw labs, consent for potential C-section. These happen together, not sequentially.

Medication Focus

High-yield OB medications — know the purpose, monitoring, and antidote

Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)

Seizure prevention in preeclampsia/eclampsia · Fetal neuroprotection before 32 weeks · Tocolytic (preterm labor)

IV — loading dose + continuous infusion. Always on a pump. Never push undiluted.

Respirations ≥12/min · Reflexes (DTRs present) · uRine output ≥30 mL/hr

Calcium gluconate 1 g IV — keep at bedside for every patient on Mag

⚠️ TOXICITY: Resp <12, absent DTRs, UO <30 mL/hr → STOP infusion + give calcium gluconate immediately
Oxytocin (Pitocin)

Labor induction/augmentation · Postpartum hemorrhage prevention (after delivery)

IV infusion — titrated to contraction pattern. Never bolus for induction.

Late decelerations · Hyperstimulation (>5 contractions in 10 min) · FHR abnormalities

Hyperstimulation · Water retention/hyponatremia · Hypotension

⚠️ NCLEX TRAP: Late decelerations → STOP Pitocin FIRST, then reposition and O₂. Students often skip stopping the Pitocin.
RhoGAM (Rho[D] Immune Globulin)

Prevents Rh sensitization in Rh– mothers exposed to Rh+ fetal blood

28 weeks · Within 72 hours postpartum · After any pregnancy loss, invasive procedure, or trauma

IM injection

Positive indirect Coombs test (mom already sensitized) — RhoGAM cannot reverse existing sensitization

⚠️ NCLEX TRAP: A positive indirect Coombs = sensitized = do NOT give RhoGAM. It prevents sensitization, not treats it.
Betamethasone (Corticosteroid)

Accelerates fetal lung maturity — increases surfactant production in preterm fetuses

Given to mother when preterm delivery is anticipated (typically 24–34 weeks)

IM to mother · 2 doses, 24 hours apart

Full effect in 24–48 hours. Reduces risk of RDS, IVH, and NEC in preterm newborn.

💡 If preterm labor + <34 weeks = anticipate betamethasone order. Also anticipate mag sulfate for neuroprotection if <32 weeks.

Case Studies

NGN-style clinical scenarios — think through each before revealing the answer

Case 1 · Preeclampsia with Severe Features

A 32-week patient is admitted with BP 162/110, severe headache, RUQ pain, and 3+ proteinuria on dipstick. Deep tendon reflexes are 3+. The provider orders IV magnesium sulfate. One hour into the infusion, the nurse assesses: respirations 9/min, DTRs absent, and urine output 18 mL/hr over the past hour.

Establish a baseline of the three parameters used to monitor for mag toxicity: respiratory rate (must be ≥12), deep tendon reflexes (must be present), and urine output (must be ≥30 mL/hr). Without a baseline, you can't recognize toxicity.

✓ Baseline respirations, DTRs, and urine output

All three signs indicate magnesium toxicity (resp <12, absent DTRs, UO <30 mL/hr). Priority: STOP the infusion immediately, then administer calcium gluconate IV (the antidote). Call for help and prepare for respiratory support.

✓ Magnesium toxicity → STOP infusion + give calcium gluconate immediately

During a seizure: protect from injury (pad rails, clear environment), turn to side-lying (protect airway, prevent aspiration), call for help, and time the seizure. Do NOT put anything in the mouth. Note: calcium gluconate is the Mag antidote, not the seizure treatment.

✓ Protect from injury · Side-lying for airway · Call for help · Time the seizure
Case 2 · Obstetric Emergency Triage

Four antepartum patients present simultaneously. Patient A: 38 weeks, Braxton Hicks every 15 min, no cervical change. Patient B: 32 weeks, BP 174/116, severe headache, visual changes, RUQ pain. Patient C: 30 weeks, painless bright red vaginal bleeding (suspected placenta previa). Patient D: 28 weeks, regular contractions every 5 min, cervix dilated to 3 cm.

Patient B is the priority. BP 174/116 + severe headache + visual changes + RUQ pain = severe preeclampsia with imminent risk of eclampsia (seizure). This is the most immediately life-threatening presentation.

✓ Patient B — severe preeclampsia with imminent eclampsia risk

Vaginal examination is absolutely contraindicated with placenta previa. The placenta overlies the cervical os — any digital examination can rupture vessels and cause massive, potentially fatal hemorrhage. Bed rest, fetal monitoring, and IV access are appropriate.

✓ Vaginal exam — NEVER with suspected placenta previa

Betamethasone (corticosteroid) to accelerate fetal lung maturity, and magnesium sulfate as a tocolytic and for fetal neuroprotection before 32 weeks. Pitocin would worsen labor. Calcium gluconate is Mag's antidote, not a treatment here.

✓ Betamethasone (lung maturity) + Magnesium sulfate (tocolytic + neuroprotection)
