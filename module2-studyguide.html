🎯 The Big Picture
Pregnancy =

A NORMAL physiologic state — ask "expected or dangerous?" for every finding

Your Priority is TWO

Mom first, then fetus. A stable mom = a stable baby.

NCLEX Mindset

Recognize danger EARLY and intervene. Don't wait.

1
RH Incompatibility
The Problem
  • Rh– mom + Rh+ baby = risk for hemolytic disease of the newborn
  • Indirect Coombs = tests MOM (antibodies in serum)
  • Direct Coombs = tests BABY (antibodies on RBCs)
RhoGAM — When to Give
  • ✓ 28 weeks gestation
  • ✓ Within 72 hr postpartum if baby is Rh+
  • ✓ After miscarriage, abortion, ectopic pregnancy
  • ✓ Invasive procedures (CVS, amnio)
  • ✓ Abdominal trauma
  • ✓ External cephalic version
⚠️
NCLEX Trap!
Do NOT give RhoGAM if the mother already has a positive antibody screen. RhoGAM prevents sensitization — it cannot treat existing sensitization.
2
Hypertensive Disorders
Know the differences.
DisorderKey FeaturesClues / Notes
Gestational HTN BP ≥140/90 after 20 wks; no proteinuria No protein in urine — key differentiator
Preeclampsia BP ≥140/90 after 20 wks + proteinuria (≥300 mg/24 hr) Edema, HA, vision changes, RUQ pain
Preeclampsia w/ Severe Features Any severe feature = high risk Severe HTN, thrombocytopenia, ↑ LFTs, renal impairment, pulmonary edema, severe HA, vision changes
HELLP Syndrome Hemolysis, Elevated Liver enzymes, Low Platelets RUQ pain, N/V, malaise — may mimic GI illness
Eclampsia Seizures in a client with preeclampsia Seizure activity — most dangerous complication
Magnesium Sulfate (MgSO₄)
Purpose
Prevents seizures in preeclampsia and eclampsia
Monitor Closely
  • Respirations ≥12/min
  • Deep tendon reflexes
  • Urine output ≥30 mL/hr
Antidote
Calcium Gluconate
Keep at bedside at all times
3
Fetal Heart Rate Patterns
What the monitor is telling you.
PatternCauseMeaning / Nursing Action
✅ Early Deceleration Head compression Usually benign — no intervention needed
⚠️ Variable Deceleration Cord compression Varies in shape & timing → Reposition, O₂, IVF
🚨 Late Deceleration Placental insufficiency Begins after contraction — ominous → Reposition (left), O₂, IVF, notify provider, stop Pitocin
😊 Acceleration Fetal movement "Happy baby!" ↑ FHR ≥15 bpm for ≥15 sec = reassuring
❗ Minimal/Absent Variability Possible fetal distress Concerning — notify provider immediately
4
Obstetric Emergencies
Know these. Act FAST.
Placenta Previa
  • Placenta in lower uterine segment
  • Painless, bright red bleeding
  • Do NOT do vaginal exam
  • Notify provider — C-section likely
Placental Abruption
  • Premature separation of placenta
  • Painful, dark red bleeding
  • Rigid, tender uterus
  • Fetal distress — notify provider immediately
Umbilical Cord Prolapse
  • Cord descends past presenting part
  • Fetal distress — cord compression
  • Knee-chest position
  • Do NOT push cord back in
  • Emergency! Lift presenting part off cord
Uterine Rupture
  • Tear in uterine wall
  • Sudden, severe pain then relief
  • Loss of fetal station
  • Fetal distress
  • Emergency surgery required
5
Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM)
High-yield facts.
Risk Factors
  • Obesity
  • Age >25
  • Family history of DM
  • Previous GDM
  • PCOS
  • Ethnicity (Hispanic, Black, Asian, Native American)
Testing
  • Screen at 24–28 weeks
  • 1-hr glucose screen (≥140 mg/dL = abnormal)
  • If abnormal → 3-hr GTT (100 g glucose)
💡 NCLEX Pearl: Tight glucose control reduces risks for mom & baby.
Risks
Maternal
  • Preeclampsia
  • Polyhydramnios
  • C-section
  • Type 2 DM later in life
Baby
  • Macrosomia
  • Birth injury
  • Neonatal hypoglycemia
  • Jaundice
Treatment
  • → Medical nutrition therapy (first line)
  • → Exercise
  • → Insulin preferred if medication needed — oral meds not first line in pregnancy
6
Prenatal Care Pearls
Key things to remember.
Fundal Height Landmarks
  • 12 wks — just above symphysis pubis
  • 20 wks — at umbilicus
  • 36 wks — xiphoid process (3 fingerbreadths below)
  • Drop (engagement) before labor
Kick Counts
  • Begin after 28 weeks
  • 10 movements in 2 hours = normal
  • Call provider if fewer than 10
GTPAL

Weight Gain Guidelines

  • Normal (18.5–24.9): 25–35 lbs
  • Overweight (25–29.9): 15–25 lbs
  • Obese (≥30): 11–20 lbs

8
Vaccines in Pregnancy
✅ Safe (Recommended)
  • ✓ Tdap (27–36 weeks)
  • ✓ Influenza (injectable only)
  • ✓ COVID-19
  • ✓ RSV (32–36 weeks in season)
  • ✓ Hepatitis B (if indicated)
💡 Pearl: Discuss vaccines during pregnancy, but they cannot be given until after birth if they are live vaccines.
❌ Avoid (Live Vaccines)
  • ✗ MMR
  • ✗ Varicella (chickenpox)
  • ✗ Live Influenza (FluMist)
  • ✗ Zoster (Shingles)
  • ✗ Oral Typhoid
⚠️ Live vaccines carry risk of fetal infection — discuss timing for postpartum administration.
9
Report Immediately (Not Normal!)
!Vaginal bleeding
!Severe headache or visual changes
!RUQ or epigastric pain
!Severe swelling (face, hands)
!Regular painful contractions before 37 weeks
!Decreased fetal movement
!Leaking fluid or foul-smelling discharge
!Dizziness or syncope
!Shortness of breath / chest pain
⚡ NCLEX Rapid Fire
Leukocytosis (↑ WBC) can be NORMAL in pregnancy
Mild dilutional anemia is EXPECTED
Proteinuria is NEVER NORMAL — always report
GDM screening at 24–28 weeks — not before
Insulin = preferred GDM medication in pregnancy, not oral agents
Pregnancy is hypercoagulable → DVT risk ↑
Calcium gluconate = Mag antidote — always at bedside
Left lateral position = best uteroplacental perfusion
GBS screen at 35–37 weeks — treat positive with IV penicillin in labor
Late decels = placental insufficiency — never benign
